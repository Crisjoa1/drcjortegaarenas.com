IV hydration at your hotel or home in Mexico City

IV hydration is the delivery of fluids through a vein. Dr. Ortega-Arenas offers it at your hotel or home in Mexico City only when the prior medical assessment justifies it, because oral rehydration is the first-choice treatment. Fee: $1,800 MXN, plus the visit and the nursing fee.

How it works

  1. Mandatory prior medical assessment by Dr. Ortega-Arenas at your hotel or home. The physician arrives first and assesses whether IV hydration is indicated.
  2. If IV hydration is not clinically indicated, it is not given. In that case, only the visit and the materials used in the assessment are charged; neither the $1,800 fee nor the nursing fee is charged.
  3. If it is indicated, the physician prescribes it in writing2 and you sign the informed consent form.
  4. The physician calls the nurse, who arrives in about 1 h 30 min, depending on traffic. A licensed nurse places the IV line under the physician’s supervision. The Mexican standard NOM-022-SSA3-2012 states that infusion therapy should preferably be given by licensed health professionals.2
  5. If you prefer to avoid the wait, you can ask for the nurse to arrive together with the physician. In that case, the nursing fee is charged even if the assessment shows that IV hydration is not indicated.

When it may be indicated

Oral rehydration is the first-choice treatment for mild to moderate dehydration, even when there is vomiting.1 The IV route may be considered when an adequate attempt at oral rehydration fails, as judged at the medical assessment.1 It is not offered for wellness, hangover, energy or performance.

What is included · $1,800 MXN

  • Normal saline (0.9% sodium chloride) given intravenously.1
  • If the assessment indicates it, an antiemetic or a pain reliever may be given. Before giving it, the physician explains which one, why, and its possible side effects. Evidence on the benefit of antiemetics in adults is limited, so this is decided case by case.4
  • No vitamins, minerals or other supplements are added: there is no high-quality evidence that vitamin infusions benefit people without a specific deficiency or medical condition.5

Risks and limits

IV hydration does not replace drinking fluids when that is possible.1 It carries risks, such as phlebitis, infiltration or extravasation, infection at the insertion site and fluid overload.2,3,6 That is why it is given only if the medical assessment justifies it.

When NOT to book this service

If you have any of these signs, do not book this service: call 911 or go to a hospital emergency room: altered consciousness (drowsiness, confusion, fainting), chest pain, trouble breathing, or severe dehydration (cannot drink or keep fluids down, passing almost no urine, signs of shock).1,7,8,9

Adults only (18 years and older). We do not see pregnant patients.

Require prior assessment and may rule out the therapy

  • Known heart or kidney failure, because of the risk of fluid overload.6
This is not an emergency service. In an emergency, call 911 or go to a hospital emergency room.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a prescription?

You do not need to bring a prescription. Dr. Ortega-Arenas decides whether it is indicated during the mandatory prior medical assessment; without it, IV hydration is not given.

Why are no vitamins included?

Because there is no high-quality evidence that vitamin infusions benefit people without a specific deficiency or medical condition, and high doses may cause harm.5 That is why they are not included.

How long does it take?

If it is indicated, the nurse arrives about 1 h 30 min after the physician calls. The infusion time depends on the volume indicated at the assessment. The physician tells you the estimated time before you sign the informed consent form.

What if it is not right for me?

If the assessment shows that IV hydration is not clinically indicated, it is not given. Only the visit and the materials used in the assessment are charged; neither the $1,800 fee nor the nursing fee is charged, unless you asked for the nurse to arrive together with the physician.

What is the total cost?

$1,800 + visit fee + nursing fee. For example, with a hotel visit in Zone A and nursing during standard hours: $1,800 + $2,500 + $1,500 = $5,800 MXN.
