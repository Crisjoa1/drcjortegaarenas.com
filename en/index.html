FAQ

Common questions

How do I book?

Online at cal.com/drcjortegaarenas or via WhatsApp at +52 595 109 3969.

Do you speak English?

Yes. Bilingual care in Spanish and English for local and international patients.

Who do you see?

Patients aged 15 and older. We do not see patients under 15 years of age or pregnant patients. In those cases, please see a pediatrician or your obstetrician; in an emergency, call 911.

Where are the clinics?

At Briyam Coworking Médico, by appointment, in four locations: Del Valle, Av. Río Mixcoac 25, piso 8, Benito Juárez, CDMX; Roma, Monterrey 243, piso 5, Roma Sur, Cuauhtémoc, CDMX; Polanco, Av. Presidente Masaryk 134, piso 4, Polanco, Miguel Hidalgo, CDMX; and Santa Fe, Vasco de Quiroga 3900, Torre B, piso 5, Santa Fe, Cuajimalpa, CDMX.

Do you handle emergencies?

No. Services are strictly outpatient general medicine. In an emergency, go immediately to a hospital ER.

How long does it take to reach my hotel?

It depends on the area: about 55 minutes to Del Valle, Roma and Condesa; 60–90 minutes to Polanco; about 1 hour 50 minutes to Santa Fe, due to distance and traffic. It may be shorter. Other areas: confirmed when you request the visit. Request the visit via WhatsApp at +52 595 109 3969; you will be given an estimated time there.

Will I get documents for my travel insurance?

When treatment is indicated, a written prescription is provided for your claim; acceptance depends on your policy. The English-language Travel Clinical Summary has an additional fee ($500 digital, $800 printed).

What are the clinic hours?

Monday to Friday 5–8 pm and Saturdays 11 am–3 pm, by appointment. The 8:00 pm slot is for follow-up visits only; first visits are booked 5–7 pm or on Saturday. Telemedicine, hotel visits and house calls have their own hours (see Services).

What is the Traveler Package?

Consultation + English-language Travel Clinical Summary + medical certificate if the assessment supports it: MXN 2,200 at the Del Valle clinic or MXN 3,500 for a hotel visit (Zone A, standard hours). Other locations and times: quote via WhatsApp.

IV hydration

IV hydration
after a medical assessment

Oral rehydration is the first choice, even when there is vomiting. The IV route is considered when an adequate attempt at oral rehydration fails, as judged at the medical assessment (IDSA 2017 guideline).

Single service

  • Normal saline (0.9% sodium chloride) given intravenously
  • Antiemetic or pain reliever, only if the assessment indicates it
  • No vitamins or supplements: no high-quality evidence of benefit without a deficiency

Before you book

Risks
and limits

It carries risks, such as phlebitis, infiltration or extravasation, infection at the insertion site and fluid overload. A licensed nurse places the line under the physician’s supervision. It is not offered for wellness, hangover, energy or performance.

Call 911 for altered consciousness, chest pain, trouble breathing or severe dehydration: this is not an emergency service.

Nursing Fee

The visit is charged at the hotel or house-call rate (see Fees). It requires a mandatory prior medical assessment, a written prescription and signed Informed Consent. If the assessment shows it is not indicated, it is not given: only the visit and the materials used in the assessment are charged, not the $1,800 fee or the nursing fee. If it is indicated, the nurse arrives about 1 h 30 min later; if you ask for the nurse to arrive together with the physician, the nursing fee is charged even if the therapy is not indicated. Adults only (18 years and older).
